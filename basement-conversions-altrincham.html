Below ground

Floor area without the footprint.

On tight plots and protected streets in Altrincham and Hale, going down is often the only way to add real floor area. A basement conversion underpins the existing structure, forms a waterproof envelope and brings in drainage, ventilation, heating and power before the rooms are finished to the standard of the house above.

Our Hale basement walkthrough shows the finished result: a cinema room, a home office and a lounge beneath an existing property. It is one of several basement projects we have delivered across Altrincham, Hale and Bowdon.

What we do

How a basement gets built.

01

Structure and underpinning

The existing walls are underpinned in a controlled sequence, designed by a structural engineer and inspected by Building Control as the work progresses.

02

Waterproofing

A designed waterproofing system, typically a cavity drain membrane with sump and pump or a tanked lining, so the space stays dry for the long term.

03

Services and ventilation

Drainage, mechanical ventilation, heating, power, lighting and data brought in while the shell is open, with escape routes designed in.

04

Finished rooms

Cinema rooms, offices, gyms, lounges and guest suites, finished to match the rest of the house.

Straight answers

Questions we get asked.

Converting an existing cellar usually does not, as long as the outside of the house does not change and it is not a listed building. Digging a new basement, enlarging one, adding a lightwell or changing the external appearance usually does, and some Altrincham and Hale streets sit in conservation areas. We check this with you at cost-plan stage.

Yes. Structure, waterproofing, fire escape, ventilation, damp-proofing and electrics are all covered by the Building Regulations, and the underpinning is inspected in stages. We arrange and manage this as part of the contract.

If the house is semi-detached or terraced, or the excavation is close to a neighbour's foundations, the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 almost always applies. We advise early so notices go out before the programme depends on them.

As a 2026 working guide, £3,000–£5,000 per square metre of finished floor area, excluding VAT, professional fees and abnormals. Ground conditions, water table, access for muck away and the finish specification drive the number, so we price the actual house rather than a headline rate.
