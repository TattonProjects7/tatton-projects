Our focus

What we don't do any more.

In 2026 we closed the domestic small works side of the business. Not because we couldn't do it — because a firm juggling nine kitchens can't put its owner on your site every week, and we'd rather run three projects properly than thirty at arm's length.

So we set a floor instead of a list. Above it, we'll look at anything in our three disciplines — including a serious extension or a whole-house remodel. Below it, we'll happily point you at someone good.

How a job runs

Five stages. One number.

A written cost plan at stage two and a signed variation procedure before anyone lifts a spade. That's why our final accounts land where they were promised.

Knutsford · Complete

Stage 01 — Appraise

  1. Stage 01

    Appraise

    Site, plot or unit feasibility. Abnormals, planning risk, build cost, end value. We'll tell you when to walk away — that advice is free.

  2. Stage 02

    Cost plan

    Elemental cost plan and a full schedule of works. Priced, not guessed. This is the number you build to, and the number you settle at.

  3. Stage 03

    Consent

    Planning, building control, warranty provider, licensing and compliance. Conditions discharged before we mobilise — not after.

  4. Stage 04

    Build

    Owner-managed. Weekly programme tracker, monthly valuation, written variations only. No verbal extras, ever.

  5. Stage 05

    Hand over

    Snagging closed out, warranty and O&M packs issued, final account settled at the figure we agreed at stage two.

How a fit-out goes in

Bare shell to signed off.

A floor arrives as concrete, columns and a slab. Five stages later people are working on it — and on most of our jobs, they never left the building while we did it.

Vanguard · £2M · Complete

Stage 01 — Strip out & survey

  1. Stage 01

    Strip out & survey

    Back to shell. We measure what's actually there rather than what the drawing says, and find the asbestos, the dead services and the floor that isn't level before they find us.

  2. Stage 02

    Services first fix

    Ductwork, cable tray, sprinklers, power and data. Everything above the ceiling goes in first, because everything below it depends on getting this right.

  3. Stage 03

    Partitions & glazing

    The floor stops being a space and starts being rooms. Stud, acoustic infill, glazed screens and doors — set out from a datum, not from the wall the last contractor built crooked.

  4. Stage 04

    Ceilings & lighting

    Grid, rafts and the lighting that decides whether the floor feels expensive or feels like a call centre. This is the stage where a fit-out is won or lost.

  5. Stage 05

    Finishes & handover

    Joinery, flooring, furniture, commissioning, snagging, O&M packs. Handed over ready to work in — and on most of our jobs, handed back floor by floor while the client kept trading.

As a working range in 2026: a basic Cat A refresh runs around £45–£70 per sq ft. A standard Cat B fit-out is roughly £70–£110 per sq ft. A high-specification Cat B — bespoke joinery, feature lighting, acoustic treatment, the kind of thing we did at Vanguard — runs £110–£180 per sq ft and upwards. Anyone who quotes you a single number without seeing the floor, the building's services and the landlord's licence to alter is guessing.
