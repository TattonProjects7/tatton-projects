M&E is the biggest line, the one clients understand least, and the one where a cheap quote has almost always missed something. If one price is well under the others, that's usually where the gap is.

What moves the number

Five things that matter more than the drawing

  1. The building's existing services. Good recent M&E versus twenty-year-old plant that won't take another forty desks. The single biggest swing — and invisible on a floor plan.
  2. Whether it's occupied. Working around a live business costs more. Worth every penny versus moving your staff out, but it has to be in the budget from day one.
  3. Ceiling height and structure. Low soffits mean services fight each other. Every clash becomes a variation.
  4. Access. A city-centre floor with one goods lift and a two-hour delivery window is a different programme entirely.
  5. How resolved the design is. The one you control completely, and the one that costs most when it's wrong.

A worked example

A 220 sq m new build at a good standard: 220 × £2,300 = roughly £506,000 for the build. Add professional fees at 10–12%, planning and building control, warranty, service connections and site abnormals — and a realistic all-in figure before land is £580,000 to £620,000.

Our Knutsford new build came in at circa £400,000 for the build. A different house on a different plot with a different specification. That's exactly why a rate per square metre gets you a ballpark and nothing more.

The caveat

What a number this early is
actually worth.

01

It's a ballpark, not a price

Use these to sanity-check a budget or decide whether a project is worth exploring. Do not use them to set a figure you'll be held to, and never sign a lease on the back of one.

02

Abnormals live outside the rate

Ground conditions, drainage diversions, asbestos, structural surprises, a floor that isn't level. These are found by survey, not by a rate per square foot — and they're where budgets die.

03

Anyone quoting one number is guessing

If a contractor gives you a single figure without seeing the floor, the services and the landlord's licence to alter, it's a guess. It may be a good guess. It's still a guess, and it will move.

04

The fix is a proper cost plan

A measured survey and an elemental cost plan costs a fraction of the job. It's the difference between a number you can hold and a number that grows every month. We do this before anyone lifts a spade.
